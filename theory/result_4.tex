\section{Result 4: certifying the die-level false-alarm rate}
\label{sec:result-4}

A frozen stage-2 scorer gives every candidate a score, and a die is flagged at threshold $t$ iff
some candidate's score exceeds $t$. $p(t)$ is the probability that a defect-free die is flagged,
and $\hat p_m(t)$ the fraction of $m$ i.i.d.\ defect-free certification dies that are flagged.
The grid $t_1<\dots<t_T$ is fixed before the certification dies are drawn.

\begin{theorem}[Result 4]
\label{thm:4}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item (Hoeffding.) With probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for all $j$,
  $p(t_j)\le\hat p_m(t_j)+\sqrt{\ln(T/\delta)/(2m)}$. So the lowest $t_j$ whose right-hand side is at
  most $\alpha$ satisfies $p(\hat t)\le\alpha$ with probability at least $1-\delta$.
  \item (Multiplicative Chernoff.) Let $\eta=\sqrt{2\ln(T/\delta)/(\alpha m)}$. If $\eta<1$, the lowest
  $t_j$ with $\hat p_m(t_j)\le(1-\eta)\alpha$ satisfies $p(\hat t)\le\alpha$ with probability at least
  $1-\delta$. If $\eta\ge1$, nothing is certified.
\end{enumerate}
In both rules, if no $t_j$ qualifies, nothing is certified.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Lower-tail Chernoff bound]
\label{lem:chernoff}
If $X$ is a sum of $m$ independent Bernoulli$(p)$ variables, $\mu=mp$ and $0\le\eta<1$, then
$\P(X\le(1-\eta)\mu)\le e^{-\eta^2\mu/2}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $\lambda>0$, Markov's inequality applied to $e^{-\lambda X}$ and independence give
$\P(X\le a)\le e^{\lambda a}\,\E e^{-\lambda X}=e^{\lambda a}(1-p+pe^{-\lambda})^m\le e^{\lambda a}e^{\mu(e^{-\lambda}-1)}$,
using $1+x\le e^x$. With $a=(1-\eta)\mu$ and $\lambda=-\ln(1-\eta)$ the bound is
$\exp\bigl(-\mu[\eta+(1-\eta)\ln(1-\eta)]\bigr)$. The function $f(\eta)=\eta+(1-\eta)\ln(1-\eta)-\eta^2/2$ has
$f(0)=0$ and $f'(\eta)=-\ln(1-\eta)-\eta\ge0$, so $\eta+(1-\eta)\ln(1-\eta)\ge\eta^2/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:4}]
Fix $j$. The flags $X_i=\mathbb 1[\text{die }i\text{ flagged at }t_j]$ are independent
Bernoulli$(p(t_j))$, because the dies are i.i.d.\ and the scorer and grid are fixed.

(i) Hoeffding's inequality \citep{hoeffding1963probability} for the $[0,1]$-valued $1-X_i$ gives
$\P(p(t_j)-\hat p_m(t_j)\ge\epsilon)\le e^{-2m\epsilon^2}$. By the union bound over the $T$ grid points
the probability that some $j$ fails is at most $Te^{-2m\epsilon^2}=\delta$ at
$\epsilon=\sqrt{\ln(T/\delta)/(2m)}$. On the complementary event every $t_j$ obeys the inequality,
in particular the data-dependent $\hat t$, whose right-hand side is $\le\alpha$ by construction.

(ii) The rule fails only if some $t_j$ with $p(t_j)>\alpha$ passes, i.e.\
$m\hat p_m(t_j)\le(1-\eta)\alpha m$. Binomial$(m,p)$ is stochastically increasing in $p$ (write it as
$\sum_i\mathbb 1[U_i\le p]$ with i.i.d.\ uniform $U_i$), so for $p(t_j)>\alpha$ this probability is at most
its value at $p=\alpha$, which \cref{lem:chernoff} with $\mu=\alpha m$ bounds by
$e^{-\eta^2\alpha m/2}=e^{-\ln(T/\delta)}=\delta/T$. The union bound over the $T$ thresholds gives $\delta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Certified bound for the Chernoff rule]
Let $c=2\ln(T/\delta)/m$ and $U(\hat p)=\bigl((\sqrt c+\sqrt{c+4\hat p})/2\bigr)^2$. Since $a-\sqrt{ca}$
increases for $a\ge c/4$, $p(t_j)>U(\hat p_m(t_j))$ iff $\hat p_m(t_j)<(1-\eta_j)p(t_j)$ with
$\eta_j=\sqrt{c/p(t_j)}$, which by \cref{lem:chernoff} at $\mu=mp(t_j)$ has probability at most $\delta/T$.
So $p(t_j)\le U(\hat p_m(t_j))$ for all $j$ at once with probability at least $1-\delta$, and
$U(\hat p)\le\alpha$ iff $\hat p\le(1-\eta)\alpha$: the rule certifies exactly where this bound is below
$\alpha$. The table reports $U$ as the Chernoff rule's certified bound.
\end{remark}

\begin{inshort}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Each threshold's false-alarm count is a binomial; the threshold is chosen from the data,
    so protect all $T$ at once with a union bound. Choosing costs $\ln T$.
  \item Hoeffding: additive margin $\sqrt{\ln(T/\delta)/2m}$, the one for the worst-case variance $\frac14$.
  \item Chernoff: relative margin $\eta=\sqrt{2\ln(T/\delta)/(\alpha m)}$. At a rate near $\alpha$ the
    variance is about $\alpha$, not $\frac14$, so far fewer dies are needed.
  \item The grid must be fixed before the certification dies are seen (here: from the validation pool).
\end{enumerate}
\end{inshort}

$T=\EFourT$. Fixed before any certification die is drawn: the scorer (norm-constrained logistic

Neither rule ever selected a threshold whose true rate exceeds $\alpha$ (largest: $\EFourMaxSelectedFar$
for Hoeffding, $\EFourChMaxSelectedFar$ for Chernoff). Given the scorer, grid and far-test pool, a
rule's violation indicators over the repeats are i.i.d.\ Bernoulli$(q)$, and zero violations have
probability $(1-q)^R\le e^{-qR}<\delta$ whenever $q>\ln(1/\delta)/R$; so with confidence $1-\delta$ each
rule's violation rate is at most $\ln(1/\delta)/R=\EFourViolBoundZero$ at each $m$.

Hoeffding cannot certify anything below $m=\EFourMinMPossible$, where its margin alone exceeds
$\alpha$, and certifies in every repeat from $m=\EFourSmallestM$. The Chernoff rule needs only
$\eta<1$, i.e.\ $m\ge\EFourChMinMPossible$: it certifies in $\EFourChThreeHundredCert$ of repeats at
$m=300$ and in every repeat from $m=\EFourChSmallestMAll$. Its thresholds also land much closer to
$\alpha$: at $m=2{,}000$ the true rate is $\EFourChTwoKTrueFar$ against $\EFourTwoKTrueFar$, and
detection is $\EFourChTwoKDetect$ against $\EFourTwoKDetect$. A threshold chosen with full knowledge
of $p(t)$ would detect $\EFourOracleDetect$.
